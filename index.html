Chapter 4

CVs That Pass UK Screening

Your CV has two readers. The first is often software: an applicant tracking system (ATS) that parses your CV into fields and lets recruiters search and filter. The second is a tired human who may spend well under a minute deciding whether to read further. A good UK tech CV is written for both.

UK CV basics (that differ from India)

  • Length: aim for no more than two A4 pages. For many new graduates, one strong page plus a little is enough.
  • No photo, no date of birth: the National Careers Service says you should not include your age, date of birth, marital status or nationality.
  • No "Declaration" or "Father's name": these are common on Indian CVs and are not used in the UK.
  • Contact details: name, phone, email, LinkedIn and GitHub. A city is enough for location ("Sheffield, UK").
Tip: the "master CV" trick. Keep a long master CV with everything you have ever done. For each application, copy it and cut it down to the most relevant two pages. Tailoring becomes deleting, not writing from scratch.

Before and after: the profile

BeforeHardworking and dedicated individual seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills for the growth of the organisation and myself.
AfterMSc Computing graduate (University of Sheffield, 2026) with 2 years' backend experience in India building Java/Spring APIs for a fintech client. Recently built a real-time transport delay predictor in Python and AWS for my dissertation. Looking for a graduate or junior backend role in Sheffield, Leeds or Manchester. UK Graduate visa, right to work until [month year].

Before and after: an experience bullet

BeforeResponsible for working on APIs and fixing bugs.
AfterReduced average API response time by about 30% by adding Redis caching and rewriting two slow SQL queries for a payments dashboard used by [number] internal users.

Why it works: action verb, specific technology, measurable result, context. If you do not have an exact number, use honest approximations or describe the scale in other ways. Never invent a metric.
